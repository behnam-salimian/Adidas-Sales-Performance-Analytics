## 🎯 Executive Summary & Business Scope
This diagnostic case study analyzes **$120M in Total Revenue** and **$33M in Operating Profit** generated by Adidas across the United States retail network during **2020–2021**. 

Achieving a notable **+296% Year-over-Year (YoY) revenue surge** with an overall **28.0% Operating Margin**, the operational ecosystem reveals deep performance disparities across regional territories, sales channels, and product categories. This project evaluates multi-channel profitability, diagnoses margin variations across retail partners, and outlines data-driven strategies for inventory and partnership allocation.

## 📈 Core Financial & Operational Scorecard
* **Total Revenue:** $120M
* **Total Operating Profit:** $33M
* **Overall Profit Margin:** **28.0%**
* **Year-over-Year (YoY) Growth:** **+296%**
* **Top Revenue Region:** **West** ($36.4M | 30% Share)
* **Top Selling City:** **New York** ($5.68M)
* **Leading Revenue Category:** **Men's Street Footwear** ($28M | 23% Share)
* **Highest Margin Category:** **Women's Apparel** (**28.8%** Margin | $24M)
* **Volume Distribution Channel:** **Online** ($45M | 37% Share)
* **Most Profitable Channel:** **In-Store** ($12.76M Operating Profit)

## 🔍 Key Analytical Findings (from Case Study)

### 1. Channel Performance: Revenue Velocity vs. Profit Realization
* **Online:** Drove the largest gross volume (**$45M revenue**), but generated the lowest operating profit (**$9.66M**) due to higher fulfillment, logistics overhead, and digital acquisition costs.
* **In-Store:** Delivered the lowest top-line volume (**$36M revenue**) while yielding the highest net profit (**$12.76M**), driven by higher price integrity and lower direct fulfillment costs.
* **Outlet:** Maintained balanced throughput (**$40M revenue / $10.80M profit**), acting as an essential clearing mechanism.

| Sales Method | Revenue | Operating Profit | Profit Dynamics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Online** | $45M | $9.66M | High volume, margin dilution |
| **Outlet** | $40M | $10.80M | Balanced volume and margin |
| **In-Store** | $36M | $12.76M | Highest margin and profitability |

### 2. Regional Divergence & Metropolitan Concentration
* **West & South Dominance:** Combined, the West ($36.4M, 30%) and South ($25.1M, 21%) account for over **51%** of total business revenue.
* **The Northeast Paradox:** While **New York** was the highest-performing single city ($5.68M), the broader **Northeast region ranked lowest** overall ($16.7M, 14%), highlighting regional polarization.
* Top Metropolitan Hubs: **New York** ($5.68M), **San Francisco** ($4.93M), **Charleston** ($4.90M), and **Portland** ($4.18M).

### 3. Category & Merchandising Economics
* **Street Footwear:** Combined (Men's & Women's) accounts for **$45M (37.5%)** of total sales, representing the brand's primary volume engine.
* **Women's Apparel:** Generated **$24M** at an outstanding **28.8% operating profit margin**, standing out as the portfolio's top-tier margin contributor.

### 4. Retail Partner Contribution
* **Tier 1 Anchors:** **West Gear** ($32.40M) and **Foot Locker** ($29.00M), alongside **Sports Direct** ($24.60M), accounted for **~72%** of wholesale partner volume.
* **Underperforming Marketplaces:** **Walmart** ($10.50M) and **Amazon** ($10.00M) exhibited lower volume throughput relative to their market reach.

## 💡 Strategic Recommendations

1. **Re-balance Channel Economics (Omnichannel Alignment):**
   - Enhance the digital checkout experience to lift average order value (AOV) and reduce order fulfillment friction.
   - Leverage In-Store profitability via cross-selling, exclusive retail experiences, and omnichannel fulfillment (BOPIS).

2. **Capitalize on High-Margin Categories:**
   - Expand assortment and inventory allocation for **Women's Apparel** to scale its high-margin profile.
   - Protect Street Footwear volume from aggressive promotional dilution.

3. **Retail Partner Governance:**
   - Deepen strategic alignment with top contributors (**West Gear**, **Foot Locker**).
   - Re-evaluate contract terms and shared operational margins with lower-performing channels like Amazon and Walmart.
